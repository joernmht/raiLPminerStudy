Disruptions to high-speed train operations result in deviations from the official timetable and train delays, leading to scheduling conflicts. A conflict-free rescheduled timetable and corresponding executable speed profiles are essential to restore disrupted train operations. In this paper, we introduce an integrated framework for the train rescheduling and speed management problem (TRSMP) during disruptions caused by partial segment blockages. Under a flexible rescheduling strategy, affected trains can switch to the opposite track to bypass the blockage. A mixed-integer linear programming model is formulated to simultaneously optimize train times, orders, routes and speed profiles, aiming to minimize the total deviation time and traction energy consumption. To reduce the complexity of the integrated model, we decompose the model into a reformulated integer programming model and a series of train operation plans. This decomposition approach transforms the TRSMP into a train plan selection problem, effectively decoupling both the interactions among trains and interdependencies between rescheduling and speed management. Moreover, we develop an adaptive algorithm with acceleration techniques to speed up the solving of the reformulated model. Case studies based on the Chinese high-speed railway show that our decomposition approach provides near-optimal solutions within acceptable computational time in large-scale instances. Our integrated method outperforms the sequential optimization method in both solution time and feasibility guarantees of speed profiles. The proposed rescheduling strategy exhibits enhanced effectiveness in mitigating delays while preserving line capacity during partial blockages.

Introduction

High-speed railways offer a rapid, efficient, and eco-friendly mode of transport that significantly enhances passenger mobility and drives regional economic growth. In recent years, the high-speed railway network has been undergoing continuing global expansion, resulting in increased traffic (Dal Sasso et al. 2022). In high-speed railway operations, train scheduling, as one of the most critical and challenging tasks, aims to execute and maintain a conflict-free timetable (Schälicke and Nachtigall 2025). However, the high-speed railway transport system is inherently complex and frequently subject to the impacts of unforeseen events, rendering the established timetable inexecutable. Based on severity, sudden events can be categorized into two types: disturbances and disruptions (Cacchiani et al. 2014). Disturbances are minor operational interruptions, such as slight delays during boarding, which can generally be managed within the existing timetable due to its inherent robustness. The core issue arising from minor disturbances is railway delay management (Flamini and Pacciarelli 2008; Schachtebeck and Schöbel 2010; Pellegrini, Marlière, and Rodriguez 2014). In contrast, disruptions are major operational interruptions causing substantial timetable modifications, that can severely affect train operations, leading to delays and increased operational costs (Kroon, Maróti, and Nielsen 2015; Borraz-Sánchez, Klabjan, and Uygur 2020; Croella et al. 2022). When a disruption occurs, some trains cannot travel normally in affected segments, leading to deviations from the scheduled times and rendering predesigned speed profiles inapplicable (Bärmann, Martin, and Schneider 2021). These events necessitate a systematic adjustment of operational strategies, including arrival and departure times adjustments (retiming), train order modifications (reordering), train reoute alterations (rerouting), or even train service cancellations, referred to as the train rescheduling problem (TRP) (D’Ariano et al. 2008; Veelenturf et al. 2016). Among disruptions, those caused by partial segment blockages occur commonly (Louwerse and Huisman 2014; Zhan et al. 2016). Such disruptions are often caused by factors such as equipment failures that prevent the normal passage of one side of the track. Trains are unable to pass through specific segments (i.e., disruption areas) at specific periods (i.e., during the disruption time window). Under such disruption scenarios, the TRP can incorporate local rerouting strategies. The rerouting strategy is designed to enhance operational flexibility and maintain unidirectional line capacity, allowing affected trains to divert to the opposite track within the disruption time window, thereby effectively mitigating delays. However, this strategy increases the complexity of the problem, as it introduces potential conflicts between bidirectional trains sharing the track resources, requiring the rescheduled timetable to precisely coordinate the paths and timing of bidirectional trains.

The implementation of a feasible rescheduling timetable of the TRP depends on the driving strategies at the train control level. Speed management translates operational-level dispatch instructions into precise and feasible train driving strategies, generating corresponding speed profiles to form the train speed management problem (TSMP) (Howlett, Pudney, and Albrecht 2022). The feasibility of a rescheduled timetable is directly contingent upon the feasibility of its corresponding speed profiles. This critical dependency highlights the necessity of incorporating speed management into disruption recovery frameworks. In partial segment blockage scenarios, trains must execute driving strategies based on optimized speed profiles of the TSMP, accelerating or decelerating in real time to meet the strict arrival/departure and inter-station running times requirements of the updated timetable, while passing through conflict zones. In this process, different speed profiles give rise to significant differences in energy consumption, thereby naturally establishing energy efficiency as an important co-optimization objective. Specifically, a disruption-affected train may either accelerate to arrive at the station earlier, which could necessitate an extended dwell time due to resource conflicts, or decelerate to align its arrival with the post-disruption recovery time, where these two strategies entail significantly distinct energy consumption patterns (Zhan et al. 2022).

The TRP and TSMP are closely related as inherent interdependencies exist between traffic-related properties (e.g., train running times, arrival and departure times) and train dynamics properties (e.g., train deceleration and acceleration) (Dong et al. 2021). Addressing these two problems separately fails to capture their intrinsic connection, may lead to rescheduling timetables that are infeasible at the control level, or to speed profiles that violate operational constraints, resulting in ineffective response to events (Yang et al. 2016). Consequently, the integrated optimization of train timetables and speed profiles under disruptions has emerged as a highly complex and challenging problem, referred to as the train rescheduling and speed management problem (TRSMP), attracting growing attention in high-speed railway operations research in recent years (e.g. Xu et al. 2017; Zhou et al. 2017; Xiu et al. 2025). In the context of partial blockages, the implementation of rerouting strategies further intensifies this complexity by amplifying potential conflicts between bidirectional trains, rendering the integrated problem a critical and yet unresolved challenge in the literature. Key challenges lie in synergistically resolving bidirectional train conflicts and the coupling between train rescheduling and speed management while rigorously ensuring the conflict-free operation of trains. This study proposes a novel integrated modeling and solution framework to address the TRSMP problem in a disruption caused by partial segment blockages, aiming to support coordinated rescheduling and control while facilitating rapid service recovery. Our scientific contribution is fivefold:

We introduce an innovative approach by integrating TRP and TSMP into a single framework, making this study one of the first to tackle both aspects simultaneously in the context of disruptions caused by partial segment blockages. This framework explicitly handles the dual challenges of bidirectional train conflict resolution on a shared track and the coupled decision-making between train rescheduling and speed control. It simultaneously optimizes the train times, orders, routes and speed profiles.

We propose a mixed-integer linear programming (MILP) model that yields a conflict-free timetable and the corresponding executable speed profiles. Our formulation aligns train position and timing within the timetable with speed profiles, enhancing the accuracy and utility of the integrated model.

To tackle the computational complexity of the integrated model, we propose a decomposition-based solution method. We decompose the MILP into a reformulated integer programming (IP) model, focusing on train interactions, and a series of train plans, each addressing the operation of a single train. The integrated problem is transformed into a train plan selection problem. We have proved that the IP model with sufficiently fine interval plans is equivalent to the MILP.

We designed an adaptive iterative algorithm to address the combinatorial explosion of train plans and the potential infeasibility issues in the IP model. This algorithm iteratively refines the plan granularity within the neighborhood of the previous solution, thereby enhancing solution quality while maintaining computational efficiency. To further accelerate the process, we incorporate two tailored techniques with an adaptive refinement process and a local branching strategy.

Through extensive numerical experiments based on real-world scenarios, we validate the effectiveness of the proposed method. The results demonstrate that the adaptive algorithm accelerates the solution process for large-scale instances compared to solving the MILP or the IP models.
