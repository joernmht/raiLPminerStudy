## Appendix D: MILP model

Due to the presence of nonlinear terms in the objective function (1), we introduce auxiliary variables $\tilde{d}_{i,n}$, $\tilde{a}_{i,n}$ and $\tilde{u}_{i,k}$, satisfying $\tilde{d}_{i,n} = \left| d_{i,n} - \overline{d}_{i,n} \right|$,$\tilde{a}_{i,n} = \left| a_{i,n} - \overline{a}_{i,n} \right|$, $\tilde{u}_{i,k} = \max(0, u_{i,k})$, and introduce additional binary variables $\phi_{i,n,n',k}$ indicating whether $u_{i,n,n',k}$ is greater than 0. The TRSMP problem under partial track blockages is formulated as an MILP model (13).

$$ \begin{aligned} \min \quad & w_1 \sum_{i \in \mathcal{I}} \sum_{n \in \mathcal{N}_i^{\text{stop}}} (\tilde{d}_{i,n} + \tilde{a}_{i,n}) + w_2 \sum_{i \in \mathcal{I}} \sum_{n,n' \in \mathcal{N}_i^{\text{stop}}} \sum_{k \in \mathcal{K}_{i,n,n'}} \tilde{u}_{i,n,n',k} \times \Delta s \\ \textit{s.t.} \quad & \tilde{d}_{i,n} \ge d_{i,n} - \overline{d}_{i,n}, \qquad \forall i \in \mathcal{I}, n \in \mathcal{N}_i^{\text{stop}} \\ & \tilde{d}_{i,n} \ge -d_{i,n} + \overline{d}_{i,n}, \qquad \forall i \in \mathcal{I}, n \in \mathcal{N}_i^{\text{stop}} \\ & \tilde{a}_{i,n} \ge a_{i,n} - \overline{a}_{i,n}, \qquad \forall i \in \mathcal{I}, n \in \mathcal{N}_i^{\text{stop}} \\ & \tilde{a}_{i,n} \ge -a_{i,n} + \overline{a}_{i,n}, \qquad \forall i \in \mathcal{I}, n \in \mathcal{N}_i^{\text{stop}} \\ & u_{i,n,n',k} \ge -M(1 - \phi_{i,n,n',k}), \qquad \forall i \in \mathcal{I}, k \in \mathcal{K}_{i,n}, n, n' \in \mathcal{N}_i^{\text{stop}} \\ & u_{i,n,n'k} \le M \cdot \phi_{i,n,n',k} \ , \qquad \forall i \in \mathcal{I}, k \in \mathcal{K}_{i,n}, n, n' \in \mathcal{N}_i^{\text{stop}} \\ & \tilde{u}_{i,n,n',k} \le u_{i,n,n',k} + M(1 - \phi_{i,n,n',k}) \ , \qquad \forall i \in \mathcal{I}, k \in \mathcal{K}_{i,n,n'}, n, n' \in \mathcal{N}_i^{\text{stop}} \\ & \tilde{u}_{i,n,n',k} \ge u_{i,n,n',k} - M(1 - \phi_{i,n,n',k}) \ , \qquad \forall i \in \mathcal{I}, k \in \mathcal{K}_{i,n,n'}, n, n' \in \mathcal{N}_i^{\text{stop}} \\ & \tilde{u}_{i,n,n',k} \le M \cdot \phi_{i,n,n',k} \ , \qquad \forall i \in \mathcal{I}, k \in \mathcal{K}_{i,n,n'}, n, n' \in \mathcal{N}_i^{\text{stop}} \\ & \tilde{u}_{i,n,n',k} \ge -M \cdot \phi_{i,n,n',k} \ , \qquad \forall i \in \mathcal{I}, k \in \mathcal{K}_{i,n,n'}, n, n' \in \mathcal{N}_i^{\text{stop}} \\ & \text{Constraints (2a)–(5b), (6i)–(6q)} \end{aligned} \tag{13} $$

## 4.1 Train operation time constraints

The train schedule includes information such as departure times, arrival times and running times at each station for train services, offering critical guidance for train operations. The relevant constraints are specified in constraints (2a)–(2d):

$$ r_{i,n} = a_{i,n+1} - d_{i,n}, \qquad \forall i \in \mathcal{I}, n, n+1 \in \mathcal{N}_i \tag{2a} $$

$$ d_{i,n} \ge \overline{d}_{i,n}, \qquad \forall i \in \mathcal{I}, n \in \mathcal{N}_i \tag{2b} $$

$$ d_{i,n} - a_{i,n} \ge \overline{p}_{i,n}, \qquad \forall i \in \mathcal{I}, n \in \mathcal{N}_i \tag{2c} $$

$$ r_{i,n} \ge r_{i,n}^{\min}, \qquad \forall i \in \mathcal{I}, n \in \mathcal{N}_i. \tag{2d} $$

Constraints (2a) define the running time $r_{i,n}$ of train $i$ for the segment between station $n$ and station $n+1$. It calculates this as the difference between the arrival time at the next station $a_{i,n+1}$ and the departure time from the current station $d_{i,n}$. Constraints (2b) guarantee that the departure time $d_{i,n}$ must not be earlier than the planned departure time $\overline{d}_{i,n}$. Constraints (2c) specify that the dwell time at station $n$, which is the time difference between departure time $d_{i,n}$ and arrival time $a_{i,n}$, must be at least as long as the planned dwell time $\overline{p}_{i,n}$. The constraints ensure that if a train is planned to stop at a certain station, this stop cannot be skipped during rescheduling to guarantee passenger service. Constraints (2d) ensure that the running time $r_{i,n}$ between consecutive stations cannot be less than a predefined minimum $r_{i,n}^{\min}$.

## 4.2 Headway constraints

During train operations, to prevent conflicts among trains, the interval between departure/arrival times of two adjacent trains must exceed the headway. It is important to note that operational safety in railway systems is fundamentally guaranteed by the interlocking system. The integrated model coordinates train operations by utilizing operational headways to generate a conflict-free timetable from a mesoscopic perspective. This is detailed in constraints (3a)–(3d):

$$ a_{j,n} - a_{i,n} \ge h_a - M(1 - \lambda_{i,j,n-1}), \qquad \forall i, j \in \mathcal{I}_s, i \ne j, n-1, n \in \mathcal{N}_i \cap \mathcal{N}_j \tag{3a} $$

$$ d_{j,n} - d_{i,n} \ge h_d - M(1 - \lambda_{i,j,n}), \qquad \forall i, j \in \mathcal{I}_s, i \ne j, n \in \mathcal{N}_i \cap \mathcal{N}_j \tag{3b} $$

$$ d_{j,p} - d_{i,p} \ge h_s - M(1 - \lambda_{i,j,n}), \qquad \forall i, j \in \mathcal{I}_s, i \ne j, p \in \mathcal{P}_{n,n+1}, n, n+1 \in \mathcal{N}_i \cap \mathcal{N}_j \tag{3c} $$

$$ \lambda_{i,j,n} + \lambda_{j,i,n} = 1, \qquad \forall i, j \in \mathcal{I}_s, i \ne j, n \in \mathcal{N}_i \cap \mathcal{N}_j. \tag{3d} $$

Constraints (3a) ensure that the arrival time difference between two trains $j$ and $i$ at station $n$ is at least the minimum required headway $h_a$, adjusted by a large constant $M$ if train $i$ does not precede train $j$ at previous station $n-1$. Constraints (3b) govern the departure time difference between trains $j$ and $i$ at the same station, ensuring that it is no less than the minimum departure headway $h_d$, modified by $M$ based on the train sequence determined by $\lambda_{i,j,n}$. Constraints (3c) ensure that the time interval between two trains traversing same block section $p$ should be no less than the headway $h_s$, and that their operational order strictly adheres to the departure sequence from station $n$. The constraints prevent train conflicts during inter-station operations by enforcing temporal separation and sequencing consistency. Constraints (3d) ensure that for any two trains $i$ and $j$, one of the binary sequence variables $\lambda_{i,j,n}$ and $\lambda_{j,i,n}$ must be 1, indicating which train has the priority in departure sequence at station $n$. These constraints collectively aim to regulate the timing and sequence to prevent conflicts for trains operating in the same direction.

## 4.3 Constraints related to the selection of tracks

During the disruption period between $W_1$ and $W_2$, the track segment connecting station $n$ and station $n+1$ is blocked, as shown in Figure 1. Trains operating on the blocked track can be rerouted to the opposite track between these stations. The blocked track is reopened after the disruption ends at $W_2$. Figure 4 illustrates both feasible and infeasible timetables for a train on the blocked track. Infeasible timetables in Figures 4(a)–(d) show that the train cannot traverse the blocked track during $(W_1, W_2)$. In contrast, feasible timetables in Figures 4(e)–(f) indicate that the train either arrives at station $n+1$ before $W_1$ or departs from station $n$ after $W_2$. Binary auxiliary variables $\Omega^{\mathrm{d}}_{i,n,1}$, $\Omega^{\mathrm{d}}_{i,n,2}$,..., $\Omega^{\mathrm{s}}_i$ are used to model logical decisions based on the disruption timing and rescheduled operations.

To evaluate whether the departure time of train $i$ from station $n$ falls within the disruption time window $(W_1, W_2)$, three binary variables are introduced, where $i \in \mathcal{I}_b$, $\mathcal{I}_b$ denoting the set of trains operating on the blocked track. Variable $\Omega^{\mathrm{d}}_{i,n,1}$ checks whether the departure time from station $n$ happens after $W_1$; $\Omega^{\mathrm{d}}_{i,n,2}$ assesses whether the train departs before $W_2$; $\Omega^{\mathrm{d}}_{i,n,3}$ jointly activates when both $\Omega^{\mathrm{d}}_{i,n,1}$ and $\Omega^{\mathrm{d}}_{i,n,2}$ are triggered. These relationships are enforced through the following constraints:

$$ \frac{d_{i,n} - W_1}{M} \le \Omega^{\mathrm{d}}_{i,n,1} \le \frac{d_{i,n} - W_1}{M} + 1, \qquad \forall i \in \mathcal{I}_b, n \in \mathcal{N}_i \cap \overline{\mathcal{N}} \tag{4a} $$

$$ \frac{W_2 - d_{i,n}}{M} \le \Omega^{\mathrm{d}}_{i,n,2} \le \frac{W_2 - d_{i,n}}{M} + 1, \qquad \forall i \in \mathcal{I}_b, n \in \mathcal{N}_i \cap \overline{\mathcal{N}} \tag{4b} $$

$$ \begin{cases} \Omega^{\mathrm{d}}_{i,n,3} \le \Omega^{\mathrm{d}}_{i,n,1} \\ \Omega^{\mathrm{d}}_{i,n,3} \le \Omega^{\mathrm{d}}_{i,n,2} \\ \Omega^{\mathrm{d}}_{i,n,1} + \Omega^{\mathrm{d}}_{i,n,2} - \Omega^{\mathrm{d}}_{i,n,3} \le 1, \end{cases} \qquad \forall i \in \mathcal{I}_b, n \in \mathcal{N}_i \cap \overline{\mathcal{N}}. \tag{4c} $$

Constraints (4a) enforce $\Omega^{\mathrm{d}}_{i,n,1} = 1$ if and only if $d_{i,n} \ge W_1$, where $M$ is a sufficiently large constant for linearization. Constraints (4b) similarly activitate $\Omega^{\mathrm{d}}_{i,n,2} = 1$ when $d_{i,n} \le W_2$. The logical conjunction $\Omega^{\mathrm{d}}_{i,n,3} = \Omega^{\mathrm{d}}_{i,n,1} \cap \Omega^{\mathrm{d}}_{i,n,2}$ is implemented through constraints (4c), ensuring $\Omega^{\mathrm{d}}_{i,n,3} = 1$ when both boundary conditions are satisfied, which means the train is affected by the disruption during its journey. This triggers the train route switching mechanism as visualized in Figures 4(b)–(c).

Extending the logic from departure time evaluation to arrival time analysis at the subsequent station $n+1$, we define three companion variables: $\Omega^{\mathrm{a}}_{i,n+1,1}$ detects whether the arrival time after $W_1$; $\Omega^{\mathrm{a}}_{i,n+1,2}$ checks whether the arrival time before $W_2$; $\Omega^{\mathrm{a}}_{i,n+1,3}$ activates when both arrival conditions are met. Following the framework established for departure constraints in (4a)–(4c), the arrival time constraints are formulated as:

$$ \frac{a_{i,n+1} - W_1}{M} \le \Omega^{\mathrm{a}}_{i,n+1,1} \le \frac{a_{i,n+1} - W_1}{M} + 1, \qquad \forall i \in \mathcal{I}_b, n+1 \in \mathcal{N}_i \cap \overline{\mathcal{N}} \tag{4d} $$

$$ \frac{W_2 - a_{i,n+1}}{M} \le \Omega^{\mathrm{a}}_{i,n+1,2} \le \frac{W_2 - a_{i,n+1}}{M} + 1, \qquad \forall i \in \mathcal{I}_b, n+1 \in \mathcal{N}_i \cap \overline{\mathcal{N}} \tag{4e} $$

$$ \begin{cases} \Omega^{\mathrm{a}}_{i,n+1,3} \le \Omega^{\mathrm{a}}_{i,n+1,1} \\ \Omega^{\mathrm{a}}_{i,n+1,3} \le \Omega^{\mathrm{a}}_{i,n+1,2} \\ \Omega^{\mathrm{a}}_{i,n+1,1} + \Omega^{\mathrm{a}}_{i,n+1,2} - \Omega^{\mathrm{a}}_{i,n+1,3} \le 1, \end{cases} \qquad \forall i \in \mathcal{I}_b, n+1 \in \mathcal{N}_i \cap \overline{\mathcal{N}}. \tag{4f} $$

Constraints (4d) and (4e) are similar in structure to constraints (4a) and (4b), but focus on the arrival times $a_{i,n+1}$ at the station $n+1$. Similar to constraints (4c), constraints (4f) use $\Omega^{\mathrm{a}}_{i,n+1,3}$ to integrate the conditions set by variables $\Omega^{\mathrm{a}}_{i,n+1,1}$ and $\Omega^{\mathrm{a}}_{i,n+1,2}$ (see Figure 4(a) and 4(b)).

To address a specific case where a train’s journey overlaps with the disruption window despite departing before $W_1$ and arriving after $W_2$, we introduce a complementary logic through variables $\Omega^{\mathrm{s}}_i$. Variables $\Omega^{\mathrm{s}}_i$ activate when the train departs from station $n$ before $W_1$ ($\Omega^{\mathrm{d}}_{i,n,1} = 0$) and arrives at station $n+1$ after $W_2$ ($\Omega^{\mathrm{a}}_{i,n+1,2} = 0$). The following constraints implement this logic:

$$ \begin{cases} \Omega^{\mathrm{s}}_i \le 1 - \Omega^{\mathrm{d}}_{i,n,1} \\ \Omega^{\mathrm{s}}_i \le 1 - \Omega^{\mathrm{a}}_{i,n+1,2} \\ \Omega^{\mathrm{d}}_{i,n,1} + \Omega^{\mathrm{a}}_{i,n+1,2} + \Omega^{\mathrm{s}}_i \ge 1, \end{cases} \qquad \forall i \in \mathcal{I}_b, n, n+1 \in \mathcal{N}_i \cap \overline{\mathcal{N}}. \tag{4g} $$

First inequalities in constraints (4g) force $\Omega^{\mathrm{s}}_i = 1$ if departure times occur before $W_1$. Second inequalities similarly activate $\Omega^{\mathrm{s}}_i$ when arrival times occur after $W_2$. Third inequalities ensure $\Omega^{\mathrm{s}}_i = 1$ when both $\Omega^{\mathrm{d}}_{i,n,1} = 0$ and $\Omega^{\mathrm{a}}_{i,n+1,2} = 0$. This formulation captures trains that fully span the disruption window (as shown in Figure 4(d)). The structure intentionally mirrors but logically inverts the earlier departure and arrival constraints (4a)–(4b), (4d)–(4e), creating a cohesive detection framework.

To denote track selection decisions, we introduce binary variables $\pi_{i,n}$, where $\pi_{i,n} = 1$ indicates train $i$ must use the unblocked track between stations $n$ and $n+1$. This decision synthesizes three critical scenarios detected by prior variables: departure from station occurs $n$ within the disruption time window $(W_1, W_2)$ ($\Omega^{\mathrm{d}}_{i,n,3} = 1$), arrival at station $n+1$ occurs within the disruption time window $(W_1, W_2)$ ($\Omega^{\mathrm{a}}_{i,n+1,3} = 1$), and train spans the disruption window ($\Omega^{\mathrm{s}}_i = 1$). The logic is enforced through the following constraints:

$$ \Omega^{\mathrm{d}}_{i,n,3} + \Omega^{\mathrm{a}}_{i,n+1,3} + \Omega^{\mathrm{s}}_i \le M \cdot \pi_{i,n}, \qquad \forall i \in \mathcal{I}_b, n, n+1 \in \mathcal{N}_i \cap \overline{\mathcal{N}} \tag{4h} $$

$$ \pi_{i,n} \le M(\Omega^{\mathrm{d}}_{i,n,3} + \Omega^{\mathrm{a}}_{i,n+1,3} + \Omega^{\mathrm{s}}_i), \qquad \forall i \in \mathcal{I}_b, n, n+1 \in \mathcal{N}_i \cap \overline{\mathcal{N}}. \tag{4i} $$

Constraints (4h) represent if any variable ($\Omega^{\mathrm{d}}_{i,n,3}$, $\Omega^{\mathrm{a}}_{i,n+1,3}$ or $\Omega^{\mathrm{s}}_i$) activates, $\pi_{i,n}$ must be 1. Constraints (4i) indicates $\pi_{i,n}$ can only be 1 if as least one variable ($\Omega^{\mathrm{d}}_{i,n,3}$, $\Omega^{\mathrm{a}}_{i,n+1,3}$ or $\Omega^{\mathrm{s}}_i$) is active, preventing spurious train route switching. This formulation ensures correct train route switching: trains are routed to the unblocked track if and only if their timetable intersects with the disruption window through departure, arrival, or full-span occupancy (see Figure 4).

## 4.4 Single-track headway constraints

When a partial blockage occurs, it necessitates that bidirectional trains share the unblocked track, which requires strict adherence to scheduling constraints (5a)–(5b) to ensure headway between trains travelling in the opposite direction, as shown in Figure 1.

$$ d_{j,n+1} - a_{i,n+1} \ge h_{ad} - M(2 - \gamma_{i,j,n} - \pi_{i,n}), \qquad \forall i \in \mathcal{I}_b, j \in \mathcal{I} \setminus \mathcal{I}_b, n, n+1 \in \mathcal{N}_i \cap \mathcal{N}_j \cap \overline{\mathcal{N}} \tag{5a} $$

$$ d_{i,n} - a_{j,n} \ge h_{ad} - M(1 + \gamma_{i,j,n} - \pi_{i,n}), \qquad \forall i \in \mathcal{I}_b, j \in \mathcal{I} \setminus \mathcal{I}_b, n, n+1 \in \mathcal{N}_i \cap \mathcal{N}_j \cap \overline{\mathcal{N}}. \tag{5b} $$

Constraints (5a) ensure that when train $i$ from the blocked track arrives at station $n+1$, there must be sufficient time before train $j$ from the unblocked track departs from the same station, considering whether train $i$ has used the unblocked track earlier than train $j$ (i.e., term $\gamma_{i,j,n}$), and whether train $i$ is travelling on the unblocked track (i.e., $\pi_{i,n}$). If $\gamma_{i,j,n} = \pi_i = 1$, then the large value $M$ subtracted from the headway is reduced, tightening the constraints to ensure conflict-free operation. Constraints (5b) operate similarly to ensure a minimum headway when train $j$ from the unblocked track arrives at station $n$ before train $i$ from the blocked track has departed. If $\gamma_{i,j,n} = 1$, the constraints are adjusted to ensure that train $i$, having used the unblocked track first, does not lead to a conflict with the arriving train $j$.

## 4.5 Constraints on train speed profiles

To linearize continuous dynamics, the train’s route is partitioned into equal-length intervals, referred to as cells, as shown in Figure 3. Within each cell, the traction or braking forces and resistance are treated as constants. The line resistance in position $s$ in cell $k$ is denoted by $R_{l,k}$, and we have $R_{l,k} = R_l(s)$ for any value of position $s$. The train traction or braking force is denoted by $u_k$, and we have $u_k = u(s)$ for any value of position $s$ in cell $k$. The kinetic energy per mass unit at point $c_k$ is $E_k$. Therefore, the differential equation (6e) for the kinetic energy per mass unit of the train can be rewritten as (see Appendix B for details):

$$ E_{k+1} = \varphi_1 E_k + \varphi_2 u_k + \varphi_{3,k}. \tag{6g} $$

Equation (6g) provides a linear iterative formula for the kinetic energy per mass unit of a train on a cell. The kinetic energy $E_{k+1}$ at the next cell can be represented by the combination of the kinetic energy $E_k$, the traction or braking force $u_k$, and the line resistance $R_{l,k}$ (represented by $\varphi_{3,k}$) of the current cell. Here, $\varphi_1$ is the coefficient of the kinetic energy; $\varphi_2$ is the coefficient of the traction or braking force, which is related to the parameter $a_2$ of basic resistance and the mass $m$ of the train; $\varphi_{3,k}$ related to the line resistance $R_{l,k}$ is related to the physical layout of the cell. The expressions of the parameters of equation (6g) are shown in Appendix B. Similarly, a train reaching endpoint $c_k$ at time $t_k$ can approximate equation (6f) as (see Appendix C for details):

$$ t_{k+1} = t_k + AE_k + Bu_k{+}C\delta_k + C\delta_{k+1} + Dz_k + Dz_{k+1} + e. \tag{6h} $$

Equation (6h) is the linear expression of the time $t_{k+1}$ for the train to arrive at the next cell, as well as the arrival time $t_k$, the kinetic energy $E_k$ and traction or braking force $u_k$ in the current cell, which $\delta_k$ and $z_k$ are intermediate variables in the linearized representation of the piecewise affine function.

We define three decision variables ($E_{i,n,n',k}, u_{i,n,n',k}, t_{i,n,n',k}$) to describe the position-velocity-time-force relationship of speed profiles. The variable $E_{i,n,n',k}$ represents the kinetic energy per unit mass at cell $k$ between stations $n$ and $n'$, $u_{i,n,n',k}$ measures the traction force or braking force, and $t_{i,n,n',k}$ is the time at which the train reaches each cell. These variables align the train’s speed profile with the timetable, enabling the coordinated optimization of both train delays and traction energy consumption. Constraints (6i)–(6j) describe the linear expression of $E_{i,n,n',k}$ and $t_{i,n,n',k}$ at cell $k$ of train $i$ passing through two consecutive stations. The functions $H(E_{i,n,n',k}, u_{i,n,n',k})$ and $G(t_{i,n,n',k}, E_{i,n,n',k}, u_{i,n,n',k})$ are mapping functions of kinetic energy and the time (6g)–(6h).

$$ E_{i,n,n',k+1} = H(E_{i,n,n',k}, u_{i,n,n',k}), \qquad \forall i \in \mathcal{I}, k \in \mathcal{K}_{i,n,n'}, n, n' \in \mathcal{N}_i^{\text{stop}} \tag{6i} $$

$$ t_{i,n,n',k+1} = G(t_{i,n,n',k}, E_{i,n,n',k}, u_{i,n,n',k}), \qquad \forall i \in \mathcal{I}, k \in \mathcal{K}_{i,n,n'}, n, n' \in \mathcal{N}_i^{\text{stop}}. \tag{6j} $$

Constraints (6i) show the kinetic energy to reach the next cell $k+1$ can be derived from the kinetic energy and traction or braking force of the current cell $k$. Similarly, constraints (6j) show that the time in the next cell $k+1$ can be calculated from the cell $k$.

To ensure physically feasible train dynamics, we enforce the kinetic energy and traction or braking force boundaries through the following safety-critical constraints. Let $\mathcal{K}^1_{i,n,n'}$ denote the starting position of the cell set between stations $n$ and $n'$, and $\mathcal{K}^2_{i,n,n'}$ represent the end position of the cell set. The operational limits are implemented as:

$$ E_{i,n,n',k} = E_{\min}, \qquad \forall i \in \mathcal{I}, k \in \mathcal{K}^1_{i,n,n'} \cup \mathcal{K}^2_{i,n,n'}, n, n' \in \mathcal{N}_i^{\text{stop}} \tag{6k} $$

$$ E_{\min} \le E_{i,n,n',k} \le E_{\max}, \qquad \forall i \in \mathcal{I}, k \in \mathcal{K}_{i,n,n'}, n, n' \in \mathcal{N}_i^{\text{stop}} \tag{6l} $$

$$ u_{\min} \le u_{i,n,n',k} \le u_{\max}, \qquad \forall i \in \mathcal{I}, k \in \mathcal{K}_{i,n,n'}, n, n' \in \mathcal{N}_i^{\text{stop}}. \tag{6m} $$

Constraints (6k) indicate that the kinetic energy $E_{i,n,n',k}$ of the departure from station $n$ and the arrival at station $n'$ is equal to the minimum kinetic energy $E_{\min}$. Constraints (6l) present the kinetic energy needs to be less than the maximum kinetic energy $E_{\max}$ and greater than the minimum kinetic energy $E_{\min}$. Constraints (6m) state that the force $u_{i,n,n',k}$ should be less than the maximum traction force $u_{\max}$ and greater than the minimum braking force $u_{\min}$. To synchronize timetable rescheduling with energy-efficient speed profiles, we establish temporal coupling constraints that bind macroscopic schedule variables ($d_{i,n}$) to microscopic motion control variables ($t_{i,n,n',k}$):

$$ t_{i,n,n',k} = d_{i,n}, \qquad \forall i \in \mathcal{I}, k \in \mathcal{K}^1_{i,n,n'}, n, n' \in \mathcal{N}_i^{\text{stop}} \tag{6n} $$

$$ d_{i,p} = d_{i,n} + t_{i,n,n',k}, \qquad \forall i \in \mathcal{I}, p \in \mathcal{P}_{n,n'}, p = \mu \times k, k \in \mathcal{K}^1_{i,n,n'}, n, n' \in \mathcal{N}_i^{\text{stop}} \tag{6o} $$

$$ t_{i,n,n',k} = d_{i,n} + r_{i,n,n'}, \qquad \forall i \in \mathcal{I}, k \in \mathcal{K}^2_{i,n,n'}, n, n' \in \mathcal{N}_i^{\text{stop}} \tag{6p} $$

$$ d_{i,n} \le t_{i,n,n',k} \le d_{i,n} + r_{i,n,n'}, \qquad \forall i \in \mathcal{I}, k \in \mathcal{K}_{i,n,n'}, n, n' \in \mathcal{N}_i^{\text{stop}}. \tag{6q} $$

Constraints (6n)–(6q) are coupling constraints between train rescheduling and speed management, which define the temporal bounds for each cell between stations. Constraints (6n) represent the time $t_{i,n,n',k}$ at the starting position of the cell set between station $n$ and $n'$ is equal to the departure time $d_{i,n}$ from station $n$, ensuring speed profile initiation aligns with rescheduled departures. Constraints (6o) specifie that the departure time $d_{i,p}$ from any block section $p$ is equal to the departure time $d_{i,n}$ from station $n$ plus the time $t_{i,n,n',k}$ at the cell $k$, where $p = \mu \times k$ and $\mu$ denotes the ratio of the block section length $\Delta p$ to the cell length $\Delta s$. Constraints (6p) indicate the time $t_{i,n,n',k}$ at the end position of the cell set should be the sum of the departure time $d_{i,n}$ and the inter-station running time $r_{i,n,n'}$ between stations $n$ and $n'$. In cases where intermediate stations exist between two consecutive stops $n$ and $n'$, the inter-station running time is defined as $r_{i,n,n'} = \sum_{\tilde{n}=n}^{n'-1} r_{i,\tilde{n}}$. Constraints (6q) restrict intermediate cell arrival times to strictly comply with the temporal range. Constraints (6i)–(6q) jointly aim to control the speed curve and driving strategy to support energy-efficient operation.
